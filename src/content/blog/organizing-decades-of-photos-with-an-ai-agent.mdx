My photo archive had folders called `Camera Uploads`, `2019-and-before`, and `To Be Sorted`. Alongside them were carefully named trips, family events, and Lightroom projects. The folder names showed years of different attempts to keep the archive organized.

In July, I worked with Codex to properly organize it. The archive lived on my NAS and covered events from 1988 through 2025: family photos, iPhone uploads, files from different mirrorless cameras, exports, and the machinery Lightroom leaves behind. It added up to just over 3 TB.

We started by figuring out what was there and what I wanted to preserve. Keep each event together. Keep Lightroom catalogs independent. Preserve camera filenames and the small companion files that hold editing information. Anything uncertain could wait for me to sort later.

We did all of that on a complete copy. I've [deleted the wrong folder before](/blog/how-not-to-do-backups/). The original archive stayed untouched throughout the migration.

The inventory turned up a useful surprise: more than 40,000 files, about a third of the total, were Lightroom previews. Some previews even used the same DNG extension as original camera files. Sorting by extension alone would have mixed generated previews into the originals.

That changed how we classified the archive. Where a file lived mattered alongside its metadata. Codex mapped every old path to a proposed new location, checked for collisions, and wrote scripts that could resume after an interruption. We worked through the rules together before applying them.

The NAS needed attention too. During the copy, Docker containers were running and the little Intel Atom processor was overworked. Progress dragged. We checked the running processes and memory pressure, then stopped those services to give the migration room to work.

We migrated 119,995 files. The new structure has 310 event folders, separate areas for originals and exports, preserved Lightroom workspaces, and review folders for material we couldn't confidently place. Individual filenames stayed the same.

Here's a simplified view of the new layout:

```text showLineNumbers=false
FSIII/
|-- Events/
|   |-- 2022/
|   |   `-- 2022-total-tennis/
|   |       |-- Originals/RAW/
|   |       |-- Exports/
|   |       `-- Lightroom/
|   `-- Unknown-Date/
|-- Lightroom-Workspaces/Legacy/
|-- _Needs-Review/
|   |-- Legacy-Buckets/
|   |-- Non-Photo/
|   `-- Loose-Files/
`-- _Incoming/
```

We checked file contents with `SHA-256` hashes, then verified that the same files reached their mapped destinations. After copying and checking the data, applying the new folder layout took less than two minutes. From the start of the copy through final verification, the work took about 27 hours.

Working with an agent meant I could decide what a useful archive should look like while it inventoried the files, wrote the scripts, monitored the migration, and checked the results. When something went wrong or an assumption needed to change, we had records to inspect and a process to revise.

At the end, I had a verified archive and a clear set of decisions left to make. Lightroom still needed to be opened and relinked. Ambiguous folders still needed my attention. Keeping the original until I could do that was part of the plan.
