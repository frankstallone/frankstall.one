# Flux capacitor prop research

Research date: September 3, 2026. Goal: guide the homepage's live 3D model using
the original prop's construction and Frank's film still, including its warm light.

## What the original designer confirms

Michael Fink selected Torr high-voltage glass relays for their appearance and the
way backlighting would refract through them. He put them in an ordinary NEMA
electrical enclosure. The brief called for equipment Doc could assemble in a
garage, with imperfect edges and recognizable found parts. Fink handed off to
Michael Scheffe and explicitly says he did not complete the lighting or know
which lamps Scheffe ultimately used.

Source: [Michael Fink interview, BacktotheFuture.com](https://www.backtothefuture.com/news/2018/12/27/an-interview-with-back-to-the-future-special-effects-consultant-michael-fink).

## The enclosure

Stahlin identifies itself as the original enclosure manufacturer. Its commemorative
listing describes fiberglass. This supports a dull gray composite housing with
scuffs, rather than a machined metal case with metallic chips. The listing is a
playful, non-purchasable product page; its current dimensions and fictional
performance claims are not evidence of the original prop's precise measurements.

Source: [Stahlin, Flux Capacitor Enclosure](https://stahlin.com/products/flux-capacitor-enclosure).

## How builders reconstruct the inside

Rook's detailed replica notes distinguish the glass relays from separate
translucent, sloping acrylic light blocks underneath. They describe hidden light
sources and a sequential chase, rather than exposed LEDs inside plastic tubing.
The notes also identify automotive spark-plug leads and red elbow boots. These
are useful reconstruction observations, not production records: the author
explicitly discusses uncertainty around lamp count and layout.

Source: [Rook's Castle, Flux Capacitor Information](https://www.rookscastle.com/tutorials/time4.html).

A maker's account of a licensed Factory Entertainment prototype describes brass
cradles, an internal brass rod, and secondary black mounting brackets. The maker
states that reconstruction used film and restoration footage, without hands-on
access to the original A-car. That limits its authority on hidden construction.

Source: [Factory Entertainment prototype construction account, The RPF](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/flux-capacitor-prototype-for-factory-entertainment.346714/).

## Decisions for this model

- Model the gray enclosure as nonmetallic fiberglass; keep the gasket, fasteners,
  red embossed tape, and loose automotive leads visually separate.
- Separate the sloping acrylic light blocks from clear relay glass, brass contact
  rods, and dark brackets. Leave dark hardware visible against the illuminated
  faces. End the three arms around a small central gap.
- Use narrow amber light pools beneath the glass, with restrained spill and bloom.
  Avoid a uniformly luminous blue-white tube or a glowing center hub.
- Run a gentle inward chase over 2.1 seconds with overlapping brightness and a
  longer decay. Hover adds a little energy; click gives a brief amber surge.
- Freeze the light at a representative warm level for reduced motion. Pause
  rendering when the hero is offscreen or the tab is hidden.

The supplied film still is the visual reference for the warm amber/peach hue and
dark contact silhouettes. The amber palette and slow cadence are Frank's art
direction. This model does not claim to reproduce a measured original lamp
circuit, exact bulb count, or universal color across every film shot.
